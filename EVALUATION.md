# TRACE — Evaluation

What was measured, how, and what it does not show.

Every number here was produced by SQL in `sql/`, executed against Snowflake,
with raw output in `eval/`. Nothing is quoted from memory.

---

## 1. The problem being evaluated

Between **2025-07-01 and 2026-08-13**, the structuring threshold was raised
from ₹8,00,000 to ₹10,00,000 as an alert-efficiency measure. It was reverted on
2026-08-14 after a supervisory observation.

Customers whose weekly cash sat in the **₹8L–₹10L band during that window
generated no alert at all** — no alert, no disposition, no file. They are
unreachable by the industry-standard method for validating thresholds, which is
sampling a few hundred below-the-line alerts and extrapolating, because there is
nothing to sample.

**2,759 customer-weeks. ₹247.0 crore. Nobody looked at any of it.**

The evaluation asks one question: can the activity worth investigating be
recovered from the transaction record alone?

---

## 2. Headline result

Every one of the 2,759 weeks was adjudicated by `claude-sonnet-4-5` running
inside Snowflake via `AI_COMPLETE`. Zero parse failures.

| | |
|---|---|
| Weeks adjudicated | **2,759** (0 failures) |
| Genuinely suspicious in that population | 1,331 (48.2%) |
| **AUC on this population** | **0.608** |
| **Recall at threshold 0.45** | **0.850** — 1,131 of 1,331 recovered |
| Precision | 0.593 |
| Accuracy | 0.646 |
| Flagged | 1,910 weeks · ₹170.8 Cr · 216 customers |

Output is a three-band triage, not a verdict:

| band | weeks | notional | actually dirty | hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ESCALATE ≥ 0.60** | 687 | ₹61.4 Cr | 546 | **79.5%** |
| REVIEW 0.45–0.60 | 1,223 | ₹109.4 Cr | 586 | 47.9% |
| DEPRIORITISE < 0.45 | 849 | ₹76.1 Cr | 199 | 23.4% |

The lowest band is named *deprioritise*, not *clear*. It still contains 199
genuinely suspicious weeks. Auto-closing it would miss them.

### Lift — workload against yield

The more useful framing for an operations owner than any single metric:

| queue worked | weeks | hit rate | cumulative recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| top 18% | 492 | 0.81–0.89 | **0.301** |
| **top 25%** | 687 | 0.75+ | **0.410** |
| top 35% | 976 | 0.71+ | **0.564** |
| top 61% | 1,686 | 0.39 | 0.769 |

Top-decile hit rate is **0.889 against a base rate of 0.482** — 1.84× lift.
Discrimination is modest (AUC 0.608) but the head of the queue is materially
richer than the population, which is what makes the work finite.

### Score separation

| | n | mean p | median | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CLEAN | 1,428 | 0.482 | 0.48 | 0.18 | 0.78 |
| DIRTY | 1,331 | 0.572 | 0.58 | 0.28 | 0.78 |

Means separate; ranges overlap almost entirely. Note the ceiling: the model
never returns above 0.78 for any case, clean or dirty. Probabilities are
range-compressed, which limits how sharp the escalation band can be made.

## 3. What the comparison is, and is not

Throughout development we benchmarked the model against simulated human
reviewers. **For the invisible population that comparison does not exist.** No
analyst reviewed these weeks, because no alert was raised to review. The
baseline is not a human — it is zero.

Where a like-for-like comparison *is* possible (on alerts that did fire, which
carry human dispositions), the model does **not** dominate:

| operating point | accuracy | recall | precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human reviewers | **0.731** | 0.595 | **0.573** |
| Model @ 0.45 | 0.593 | **0.721** | 0.500 |
| Model @ 0.50 | 0.640 | 0.689 | 0.545 |
| Model @ 0.55 | 0.713 | 0.475 | **0.725** |
| Model @ 0.60 | 0.713 | 0.393 | **0.800** |

There is no threshold at which the model beats reviewers on both precision and
recall. It trades one for the other. **AUC on this population is 0.541** —
barely above chance.

The system's value is not that it adjudicates better than an analyst. It is
that it adjudicates **a population no analyst could reach**, where it performs
better (AUC 0.608) because that band is enriched with the sudden-onset accounts
a structuring operation produces — and ranks it well enough to make the work
finite.

**These are two different distributions and the numbers are not
interchangeable.** Earlier drafts of this document quoted the alert-population
AUC as though it described performance on the invisible population. It does
not.

## 4. The corpus

Fully synthetic. 1,200 customers, **607,307 transactions**, 4,856 alerts,
Jan 2025 – Sep 2026. Generated by `generator/generate.py` (seed `20260916`,
deterministic).

Four archetypes: `CLEAN` (78%), `CASH_BUSINESS` (12%), `MULE` (7%),
`LAYERING` (3%). The latter two are ground-truth suspicious.

### Two earlier corpora failed, and the failures are instructive

**v1 had no learnable signal.** `CASH_BUSINESS`, `MULE` and `LAYERING` all drew
declared income from the same distribution, so "cash versus declared income" —
the feature intended to discriminate — was identical for guilty and innocent.
Given that corpus, `llama3.1-8b` called 100/100 alerts suspicious and
`claude-sonnet-4-5` called 78/100. Neither was wrong to. κ ≈ −0.01.

**v1 also rigged the human comparison.** Reviewer labels were derived from the
archetype directly — humans saw ground truth through fog while the model saw
only features. Any model/human number from that design was meaningless.

**v2 separated the classes too cleanly.** Six independent behavioural signals
each pointing the same way produced an oracle ceiling of 0.998 and simulated
humans scoring 0.986. Nothing left to earn.

### v3: behaviour, not labels

Classes differ by what is observable in the transaction record:

| | legitimate cash trade | structuring |
|---|---|---|
| declared income | tracks turnover | unrelated to turnover |
| deposit rhythm | steady, week after week | bursts |
| deposit amounts | vary naturally | cluster just under round numbers |
| funds | retained to pay suppliers | moved out within days |
| branches | usually one | often several |

Occupation is **informative but not determinative** — 42% of mules hold
cash-trade occupations, 28% of genuine cash businesses are professionals. In v1
the mapping was perfect, which made occupation a label proxy and taught the
model to reason backwards from it.

### Behavioural mimicry — the source of irreducible error

A share of accounts draw their entire behaviour from the *opposing* archetype:

| | rate |
|---|---|
| `MULE` behaving as a legitimate business ("clean skins") | 22% |
| `LAYERING` behaving as a legitimate business | 20% |
| `CASH_BUSINESS` behaving like a mule | 12% |

**These cannot be classified correctly from the record by any reader, human or
model.** That is why launderers recruit clean skins, and it is what caps the
oracle ceiling below 1.0. It is also where a calibrated system should abstain
rather than guess.

Evidence separation by class confirms the overlap is real — means separate,
ranges do not:

| archetype | n | mean | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CASH_BUSINESS | 4,091 | 0.325 | 0.188 | **0.857** |
| LAYERING | 726 | 0.568 | 0.223 | 0.887 |
| MULE | 1,150 | **0.574** | **0.219** | 0.905 |

### The reviewer noise model

Human dispositions are **not** derived from the archetype. Reviewers read the
same evidence score the features encode, perturbed in logit space:

```
noise_sd = REVIEWER_NOISE_BASE × (MEDIAN_SKILL / skill) × (1 + FATIGUE_NOISE_GAIN × fatigue)
called   = sigmoid(logit(evidence) + N(0, noise_sd)) > (0.50 − reviewer_bias)
```

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| `REVIEWER_NOISE_BASE` | 0.70 |
| `MEDIAN_SKILL` | 0.80 |
| `FATIGUE_NOISE_GAIN` | 0.55 |
| `FATIGUE_SATURATION` | 12 alerts/reviewer/day |
| `DECISION_THRESHOLD` | 0.50 |
| reviewers | 8, skill 0.66–0.91, bias −0.11 to +0.13 |

### Resulting corpus properties

| | value | target |
|---|---|---|
| Base rate (alerts truly suspicious) | 0.317 | — |
| **Oracle ceiling on the evidence** | **0.868** | 0.85–0.95 |
| Human accuracy vs truth | 0.731 | 0.72–0.85 |
| Human recall vs truth | 0.595 | 0.55–0.78 |
| Human precision vs truth | 0.573 | realistically poor |

The ceiling is what a *perfect* reader of the record could achieve. The 14-point
gap between it and human performance is the headroom the system competes for.

**Enrichment caveat:** 31.7% of alerts here are truly suspicious versus roughly
2% in production AML. The corpus is deliberately enriched so a demonstration
does not need 50M transactions to surface three cases. This inflates absolute
escalation rates. It does not affect the recall comparison or the ranking
metrics, which are the claims made.

## 5. The replay engine

The invisible population has no `DECISION_FEATURES` — no alert fired, so
nothing was snapshotted. `CORE.V_POINT_IN_TIME_FEATURES` reconstructs, from
`CORE.TRANSACTIONS`, what a reviewer *would* have seen for any (customer, week).

Every rolling feature is computed over the 26 weeks **strictly before** the week
in question (`ROWS BETWEEN 26 PRECEDING AND 1 PRECEDING`). Allowing the current
week into its own history is how a replay flatters itself.

### Validation against the generator (4,856 alerts, both available)

| feature | correlation | median abs. diff |
|---|---|---|
| aggregate amount | **0.9998 exact match** | — |
| cash vs monthly income | **1.000** | 0 |
| weeks with cash activity | **1.000** | 0 |
| deposits just under round | **1.000** | 0 |
| weekly volatility | 0.999 | 0.01 |
| distinct branches used | 0.992 | 0.00 |
| outward transfer ratio | 0.912 | 0.07 |
| prior alert count | **−0.014** | — |

`outward_transfer_ratio` diverges by design: the generator stored an intended
per-customer constant, the engine measures transfers that actually occurred.
**The engine is the correct side** — a reviewer sees the record, not a
parameter.

`prior_alert_count` at −0.014 was a **generator bug the engine caught**: a
`cumcount()` computed on a time-sorted copy, then indexed positionally against
the unsorted frame, so every alert received another alert's count. Fixed in
`generate.py`; the engine is authoritative.

Coverage: **2,759 / 2,759** invisible weeks have reconstructed features.

## 6. Calibration

`claude-sonnet-4-5` via `AI_COMPLETE`, `temperature = 0`, JSON-schema
`response_format`, on 150 held-out alerts never used for prompt development.

Determinism is deliberate. Self-consistency sampling would give a better
uncertainty estimate, but a regulatory replay that returns different verdicts on
re-run is not defensible to an examiner.

**AUC = 0.541** on the alert population, **0.608** on the invisible population.

An earlier measurement of 0.699 was **inflated and has been discarded**. It used
generator-computed features, one of which — `outward_transfer_ratio` — was the
same simulation parameter that drives the ground-truth evidence score. It is not
observable in a bank. Scoring against it leaked the generative process.

Threshold sweep on the alert population (the operating point is chosen by us,
not by the model):

| threshold | accuracy | recall | precision | % flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.40 | 0.420 | 0.984 | 0.411 | 97.3% |
| **0.45** | 0.593 | **0.721** | 0.500 | 58.7% |
| 0.50 | 0.640 | 0.689 | 0.545 | 51.3% |
| 0.55 | 0.713 | 0.475 | 0.725 | 26.7% |
| **0.60** | 0.713 | 0.393 | **0.800** | 20.0% |

**0.45 is locked** as the review threshold and **0.60** as the escalation
threshold. Because probabilities are persisted rather than verdicts, either can
be re-chosen from this sweep without re-running a single model call.

### A fix that was right on principle and neutral on the metrics

`outward_transfer_ratio` is computed over the preceding 26 weeks. For a
sudden-onset account — no cash for 26 weeks, then a large burst — the
denominator is zero, and the original code coalesced that to `0`. The prompt
then rendered it as *"0% of deposited cash transferred out"*.

The model read that as evidence of funds being **retained**, and cited it as a
mitigating factor on the highest-scored case in the entire run. An absence of
data was presented as a measurement, and the model reasoned from it.

This was caught by **reading an evidence pack**, not by any metric. Discovery is
documented in §8.

The field now returns `NULL`, and the prompt says *"not measurable — no cash
activity in the preceding window"*. Re-running the full adjudication after the
fix:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Recalibration AUC (n=150) | 0.604 | 0.541 |
| Adjudication recall @0.45 | 0.856 | 0.850 |
| Precision | 0.596 | 0.593 |
| ESCALATE hit rate | 0.800 | 0.795 |

**It did not help.** Every figure moved marginally down. The adjudication deltas
are under 0.01 on n=2,759 — noise. The AUC change is ~1.4 standard errors on
n=150 and is not distinguishable from chance.

The fix was kept regardless. A system whose evidence packs tell an examiner
"0% transferred out" about an account with nothing to transfer is making a false
statement, and that is disqualifying independent of AUC. Recording this because
a team optimising for a leaderboard would have reverted it.

## 7. Known limitations

**The model does not beat human reviewers head-to-head.** §3. It trades
precision for recall.

**AUC is 0.608 on the invisible population and 0.541 on alerts.** The oracle
ceiling is 0.868. Substantial signal in the record is not being extracted, and
on the alert population the model ranks barely better than chance.

**The deprioritise band contains 199 genuinely suspicious weeks** (23.4%).
It is a ranking, not a dismissal.

**Probabilities are range-compressed.** Across the whole adjudication the model
never exceeded 0.78 for any case, clean or dirty, and rarely went below 0.25. It does not express strong
confidence, which limits how sharp the escalation band can become.

**Clean skins are not detected, by design.** The model scores them 0.480 against
0.447 for genuinely clean accounts — indistinguishable, which is correct. They
are irreducible, not a bug to fix.

**The cheap tier does not work.** `llama3.1-8b` failed twice: `AI_COMPLETE` with
a JSON-schema `response_format` returned NULL for 100/100 and then 139/150 rows;
`AI_CLASSIFY` returned a VARIANT whose shape did not match the assumed
`{"labels": [...]}`. No cascade is claimed. It is not needed at this scale —
SQL bounds the population to 2,759 weeks, so frontier-only adjudication costs
roughly **$15** for the entire lookback. The cascade would matter for a bank
running 200,000 alerts a year, and remains unproven.

**Synthetic data.** Because the corpus is generated, ground truth is known and
`accuracy vs truth` is computable. In production only agreement with human
dispositions would be available. Every "vs truth" figure here depends on the
corpus being a fair model of the domain — that is an assumption, argued in §4,
not a result.

## 8. Evidence packs

Adjudication produces a ranked list. A ranked list is not actionable: an
examiner asks why this case, what rule applied at the time, what was visible
then, and how they know the record has not been edited since.

**687 packs** were built for the escalation band, covering **111 customers** and
**₹61.4 crore**. Each contains the case, the finding with both sides of the
model's reasoning, the policy version in force at the time *and* today (each
resolved by temporal join, with citation), the arithmetic of why no alert fired,
the point-in-time evidence snapshot, and provenance.

Packs are hash-chained: `chain_hash(n) = SHA2(chain_hash(n−1) || content_hash(n))`.
Editing, removing or reordering any pack breaks every hash after it.
`AUDIT.V_CHAIN_VERIFICATION` recomputes the chain from the payloads and returns
a verdict rather than an assertion.

```
chained 687 packs, head=a0d16fadeae3ba73350d22606eacf3b7b8efdf3b01a4fbf1b2e401496a916e3e
verdict: INTACT   (0 payloads tampered, 0 links broken, 0 hashes mismatched)
```

An empty table returns `EMPTY — NOTHING TO VERIFY`, not `INTACT`. False
assurance is the one failure mode an integrity check must not have.

### The packs caught a defect the metrics could not

The `outward_transfer_ratio` bug in §6 was invisible to every aggregate we
tracked. AUC 0.604 and recall 0.856 looked like ordinary model limitations. It
surfaced only on reading one case, where the model's mitigating argument was
that funds had not been moved out — on an account that had no prior funds to
move.

A probability is unfalsifiable. A pack that states its evidence and its
reasoning can be checked, and checking it found an error in our own feature
engineering. That is an argument for the artifact, not an anecdote about it.

### An open provenance gap

Nothing currently prevents a pack pairing evidence at feature-version *N* with
reasoning generated at version *N−1* — which is exactly what happened between
the fix and the re-run. The provenance block records the model and thresholds
but no feature-schema version, so the inconsistency was invisible to the system
and caught only by eye. A hash of the feature view definition, recorded on both
the adjudication and the pack and checked before a pack is built, would convert
"we noticed" into "it cannot happen". Not yet implemented.

## 9. What SQL found without the model

The invisible population concentrates in two of eight branches — Karol Bagh
(22.5%) and Surat Ring Road (19.9%), **42.4% between them**. That is visible
from deterministic SQL with no model involved.

The model's escalations concentrate slightly more tightly, but only in one of
the two:

| branch | % of escalations | % of population | lift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Karol Bagh | 29.5% | 22.5% | 1.31× |
| Surat Ring Road | 20.2% | 19.9% | 1.02× |

The generator does cluster roughly half of all mules into these two branches,
and branch identity appears nowhere in the prompt. But **the model did not
discover that pattern** — one branch shows modest lift, the other none. The
apparent concentration is largely inherited from a population that was already
concentrated.

Stated because the opposite claim would have been an attractive one to make.

## 10. Reproducing

```bash
uv run --with numpy --with pandas generator/generate.py --scale slice
```

Then, in order, against Snowflake:

| script | purpose |
|---|---|
| `sql/01_foundation.sql` | bitemporal schema |
| `sql/02_eval_tables.sql` | ground-truth tables (isolated from the agent role) |
| `sql/11_reload_and_score_v5.sql` | stage and load the corpus |
| `sql/12_replay_engine.sql` | point-in-time features + validation gate |
| `sql/13_adjudicate.sql` | recalibration, then full adjudication |

Scripts `03`–`10` are superseded and retained as history: they record the two
corpus failures and the measurement error described above.

Raw outputs are in `eval/`.

**Environment:** Snowflake Enterprise, `AWS_US_WEST_2`. Models:
`claude-sonnet-4-5` and `llama3.1-8b` via Cortex `AI_COMPLETE`. All inference
runs inside Snowflake; no data leaves the account.
